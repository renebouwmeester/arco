Besides Roon, Arco can now play Apple Music straight to a streamer on your network, or clock a DAC on your Mac. Roon is optional.

Three outputs

  • Local, Roon and UPnP side by side in the menu, each with its own switch. Switch off what you don't use and it disappears from the menu
  • Roon is off on a fresh install and stays on for everyone who enabled Arco in Roon before. Without Roon, Arco doesn't look for a Core at all
  • The menu says where the music goes and shows the bit depth next to the rate

UPnP and OpenHome (Experimental)

  • Arco sends the Music app to a UPnP or OpenHome streamer, without Roon: one stream per track, each with its own title, artist, album, cover and exact length — your streamer shows them, with a true progress bar
  • Gapless: the next track is handed to the streamer ahead (UPnP's SetNext, or OpenHome's own playlist), at its own rate — 44.1 up to 192 kHz, bit-perfect
  • Sonos shows up by its room name, a stereo pair once. Sonos plays 48 kHz at most: Arco converts higher rates down to 44.1 or 48 for Sonos only, by a whole factor
  • Tested on KEF LS60, Meridian Ellipse, Naim Mu-so 2, WiiM Mini, a Sonos Era 100 pair, OpenHome (upmpdcli) and GentooPlayer. Other streamers: please tell us how it goes
  • Tips: set the streamer's volume to fixed, keep the Music app at 100, and prefer a wired streamer for 96 and 192 kHz

FLAC to your streamer (Experimental, off by default)

  • Arco can send FLAC instead of WAV to a UPnP streamer — bit-exact, encoded as the streamer fetches it. Some streamers show more about the track, or handle it better over wifi
  • To try it, in Terminal: defaults write nl.renebouwmeester.arco UPnPFLAC -bool YES and play the next track. Back to WAV: defaults delete nl.renebouwmeester.arco UPnPFLAC
  • Tested on the Naim Mu-so 2 and the KEF LS60. We'd love to hear how it goes on yours — with your log

Local (Experimental)

  • The Music app plays to a DAC on this Mac, and Arco only sets the DAC's rate for each track: from the Music app's label, before the first note, never in the middle of a track. No Roon, no Arco driver needed

Roon

  • A change of rate on a slower Mac: the Music app's short pause while Arco changes the clock now stays with Arco, so Roon no longer stops on it
  • Pause, skip to another track, play: Roon no longer plays a few seconds of the track you skipped away from, and the new track gets its own rate

macOS 15 Sequoia

  • The clock on time at a change of track, from the line the Music app on Sequoia does write

Also

  • A pause by the Music app itself (another device took your Apple Music stream, AirPods off) stops writing at once
  • The transition line in the log says when the change was and when the endpoint took it, and speaks for Arco's side only at a change of rate
